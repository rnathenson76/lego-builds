## 5. Build approach for the trailer walls: one decision to revisit

The trailer is **mostly red**, with horizontal bands at **1-plate resolution**
(the 1-plate pinstripes).

- **Option A: SNOT tiles** (the current plan). The tiles face outward, so bands
  come in whole-stud steps of 8 mm (2.5 plates). The pinstripes would need
  sideways plates or 1 × N tiles, and there are about **250–300 tiles per
  side**.
- **Option B: stacked bricks and plates.** The wall is built the normal way
  (studs up), and the outside is the smooth side faces of the bricks. Every
  plate layer can be any color, so the 1-plate pinstripes fall out
  naturally. The seams between layers also look like the ribbed walls of a real
  reefer, which you can see in photos 1 and 2. You'd need about 110 bricks
  (mostly red 1 × 8) plus 1 × 8 plates for the stripes on each side. It needs no
  tiles except on the roof.

**Recommendation: B.** It gives smooth walls, the stripes are exact, it looks
more like the real trailer, and it probably needs fewer parts. It does change
what you told me earlier ("smooth tiles"), so this is your call.

## 6. Mechanisms: how each one fits

- **Steering:** a gear rack sits in front of the steer axle. A vertical shaft
  runs up through the cab's back wall to a knob on the Aerodyne roof pod. The
  shaft has to split where the cab tilts.
- **Tilting cab:** the hinge sits at the front of the frame, at about x 2 and
  14 plates high. The cab latches at the rear.
- **Walking beam:** the two drive axles sit on one beam per side, pivoting at
  x 24.5.
- **Fifth wheel and kingpin:** a turntable or pin, so the trailer lifts
  straight off.
- **Landing gear:** legs that slide or swing down.
